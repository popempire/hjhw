We have become so fixated on the recent rapid developments in machine consciousness
that we have neglected the real problem,
which is the stagnation in the growth of human consciousness.

Consider the grand height to which we have ascended from a meta-historical perspective:
our latest human generations have been living through the birth of a planetary consciousness,
a world mind.
Envioronmentalism and ecumenical humanism are indeed religious miracles,
though their detractors and hypocritical followers have sometimes made them appear hokey.

  > *A.I* (artificial intelligence?)
  > a singular consciousness which spawned and entire race of machines
  > — *The Matrix*

It seems that humans have figured out how to bottle cleverness.
They have not yet figured out how to bottle soul.
This is the state of the discussion in the early 21st century.

What expenditure of energy is worth it
in order to offload the burden of thinking?

Is thinking what AI can do for us?

How much of mental work is thinking?

### 2026.10.11

*AI is in the news for shaking up the entire academic world of mathematics*

*Frontier models have recently produced solutions to or progress on*
*hundreds of open problems in advanced mathematics*

Whether what these machines do is *thinking* is becoming an irrelevant question.
Whether they are conscious may become irrelevant next.

They are extremely rapidly becoming very good at something
which would require a great deal of thinking for humans to do.

I feel that the conversations around AI among artists, musicians, and so-called creatives
have generally lacked nuance and seriousness.
There is reactionary fear and glib celebration,
but not a lot of articulate explorations of the ground between these poles.

I think that musicians will soon face a come-to-god moment like many mathematicians
are experiencing now. We should be serious about the likelihood that some form of
AI will soon be as good as, and then better than, we are at many of the creative
tasks we perform.

I hear the reactions in my mind. 
But AI is soulless! It can't truly express feelings!
It's just stealing and regurgitating the ideas and work of real human artists!

These objections deserve thoughtful answers, but they will not hold up under scrutiny.

Perhaps there is indeed some element of human musicality which AI will never
be able to beat us at, but it will have nothing to do with cleverness or prowess.

And after all, is AI not another human tool?
It did not come out of nowhere.
Indeed, it came out of a very specific cultural and technological situation.

Herein lies the problem with AI as we are seeing it develop:
in the cultural biases that we are building into it,
and the technological shortcomings that we are still subject to.

Commercial art was already being driven to converge towards a point of
flawless soullessness which is currently the hallmark of AI-generated art.
As long as people still line up to consume such material en masse,
it will continue to be generated,
for cheaper and cheaper.

The capabilities of generative AI 
(and hopefully the aesthetic demands of the consuming public)
are quickly surpassing this baseline.
The specifics of how this looks in the future is of concern to us now.

Right now, the machines, the tools, are being built to serve the needs
of surveillance-state techno-capitalism.
This is blatantly obvious and no conspiracy theory.
People are living through this and doing basically nothing about it,
because they feel (perhaps rightly) that there's nothing they really can do about it.
The Matrix is being built and the humans are saying,
I'm gonna invest in the Matrix!
The only way the venture capital bets being placed on AI infrastructure pay off
is if AI comes to absolutely dominate the entire economy,
and do so in a way that can narrowly benefit a few critical mega-corporations.

What would the machines look like if they were built to be tools for artists?
(Rather than to collect data on artists,
or for artists to use in service of techno-capitalism?)

Here I am actually optimistic,
because I don't think there is a way that we can build a tool that is 
sufficiently capable to achieve the kinds of disruptive intellectual labor
that funders are expecting it to
that cannot simultaneously be capable of being put to incredible new artistic purposes.
Give the artists enough time to use it,
and they will create beautiful and inspiring things.

Yet this picture still involves a human artist, a human soul at the epicenter
of an artistic creation's radiating out.
What about artistic creations that are some generations removed from any human
input, generated only from a long series of machine decisions?
or where the human impetus was generic, pedestrian, vulgar 
(perhaps a prompt like, Make some good music)?

How much does the question change, 
or how much does the emotional urgency of the question change,
if we simply do not care who gets credit for an intellectual achievment
such as composing a musical work?
Is there perhaps something inherent to ego or the celebration personal achievment
which are fundamental to the human process of music?

If thinking is not after all what humans are uniquely good at,
is it perhaps *dreaming*?
