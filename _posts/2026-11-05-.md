### future live performance ideas

I have no live shows coming up, and I have greedily desired this circumstance as an opportunity to clear my mind and conceive afresh of my approach to live musical performance. 

Running sound at the Loon— this artist, Rooster Blackspur, has given me a single channel, guitar & vocsls together, a direct out from their Fishman amp. She is using the amp exclusively as her own monitor, and I put her XLR channel in the house dry. She has pedals for the guitar and reverb already in her signal.

Obviously, this makes everything incredibly simple (and easy as long as it sounds good) for me as a sound person. 

At my last show, I played with Jake at Cousin Jeffrey's Honky Tonk. Harris sang 3 songs with us. Jake played guitar, and I alternated between piano and guitar (both thru my Blues Jr). We had beats (for all but 2 songs) on a single XLR from a laptop + interface.

We were sandwiched between Rae Fisher and the Sean Geil band on the bill. It was a fantastic night, good vibes, one of my favorite shows in a while. These were my technical debrief notes:

- packing up all of my gear to get off stage after the set felt burdensome and stressful. particularly the piano. 
- the level of the beats was too low in the house for the first couple songs, and too low in the monitors the whole time. the sound person commented that the levels of the beats seemed inconsistent from song to song, in the bass at least.
- i brought my RCF speaker system in case we needed them for the beats, but wound up leaving it in the car, since the house system seemed to have plenty of power. however, i wound up not getting quite a satisfactory mix of beats on stage. there was enough raw power, but we didn't achieve the monitor mix or arrange the monitors (e.g. turn the drum wedge towards the front) in the ideal way. this is because we didn't have sufficient time (no full sound check, just a line check before our set, and it took me a while to get everything set up).

It strikes me that I could in theory hedge against these difficulties by streamlining my setup and combining all my signals into one channel. A sound person would just mix my vocal to the appropriate level, and everything else would automatically be sorted. 

I would probably skip the amp in this setup, run guitar (and piano?) into the interface. If I brought my own monitoring, I could be self-sufficient, have nothing to fear from limited monitoring situations. Ideally, this setup could function as a hybrid PA/monitoring — i.e. I would bring a speaker system (my Bose S1, RCF, or JBL) and position it such that I could hear it and also project into an audience.

- Guitar
- Pedalboard (w/ SansAmp and vox pedal)
- Vocal mic
- Rack case w/ laptop & interface 
- PA speaker 

The above setup seems pretty manageable, but it lacks the piano. Is this an acceptable tradeoff? Would a USB MIDI keyboard be a better alternative? (would it be better to abandon the guitar and use solely piano instead!?)
